# Import, Export, Transit, and Logistics Blueprint

## 1. Confirmed operating model

Global Transport Services will use two related but separate operational areas:

1. **Trips**
   - Used for domestic fleet transport.
   - Represents a vehicle movement performed by a driver and truck.
   - Keeps the existing transport, payment, delivery-note, maintenance, and trip-report behavior.

2. **Shipments**
   - Used for freight-forwarding and clearing-agent work.
   - Covers:
     - Sea import
     - Sea export
     - Air import
     - Air export
     - Road transit
     - Logistics jobs
   - Represents the customer freight job, not one vehicle movement.
   - May contain multiple containers, packages, house bills, legs, documents, charges, vendor costs, and transport legs.

A shipment may create or link to one or more domestic Trips when GTS performs the local delivery or collection. A Trip must never be required for an international shipment that is handled entirely by external carriers.

### Vendor finance slice

Import and export shipments have a branch-scoped accounts-payable workflow:

1. Save an import/export shipment with a vendor assignment; the system automatically creates one linked AP draft for each assignment.
2. A missing cost or estimate produces a zero-value provisional draft; estimated costs also remain provisional. Enter a positive final cost on the shipment before approving the bill.
3. Capture the supplier invoice number, invoice date, and due date on the assignment. The linked draft and downloadable AP voucher are updated automatically; upload the supplier's original invoice separately when received.
4. Record one or more payments against an approved bill. Payment amounts cannot exceed the outstanding balance; the bill becomes `partially_paid` or `paid` automatically.
5. Review all branch vendor bills at **Accounts payable** (`/accounts/payables`), with search and status filters.

Vendor bills and payments are separate from domestic trip payments. The implementation is restricted to `import_sea`, `export_sea`, `import_air`, and `export_air`, and all shipment, vendor, bill, and payment queries remain branch-scoped.

## 2. Current system assessment

The current application already contains useful foundations:

- `TransportRecord` for domestic trips.
- A `ShipmentController` with shipment types:
  `import_sea`, `export_sea`, `import_air`, `export_air`, `logistics`, and `transit`.
- Customer and sales-person selection.
- Basic BL document upload.
- Charge master with buy rate, sell rate, VAT, and government-charge flags.
- Shipment charge lines and basic buy/sell/VAT totals.
- Vendor management.
- Branch-aware models and branch filtering behavior.
- Existing payment, voucher, delivery-note, and trip-report modules.

The shipment module has grown beyond its original starting form: vendor assignments,
customer invoices and receipts, vendor bills and payments, shipment status history,
invoice adjustments, and finance statements are already implemented. It is still
not a complete forwarding system. The main remaining gaps and hardening work are
recorded in Sections 20 and 21; in particular, shipment rows still share
`transport_records` with domestic Trips, appointment scheduling and container
history are not complete workflows, and posted financial records need stronger
retention and audit guarantees.

## 9. Commercial charges and profitability

The charge master remains the source for standard charges. Shipment charges should be divided into:

1. **Customer revenue**
   - Sell amount
   - VAT
   - Currency
   - Invoice status

2. **Vendor or operational cost**
   - Buy amount
   - Vendor
   - Vendor invoice/reference
   - Payable status

3. **Government or pass-through charges**
   - Duty
   - Port fees
   - Customs fees
   - Taxes
   - Supporting document

Shipment profitability should calculate:

```text
Revenue excluding VAT
- Vendor and operational costs
- Government charges treated as cost
= Gross profit
```

The system must preserve both transaction currency and branch reporting currency:

- Transaction currency
- Exchange rate
- Base-currency buy amount
- Base-currency sell amount
- Base-currency profit

Totals must be recalculated server-side whenever charge lines change. Client-side totals are display-only.

## 13. Roles and approval flow

The confirmed responsibility model is:

1. **Sales**
   - Create customer opportunity or quotation.
   - Create shipment draft.
   - Enter customer and routing information.
   - Prepare proposed revenue charges.

2. **Operations**
   - Verify shipment information.
   - Manage bookings, documents, milestones, customs, and deliveries.
   - Request vendor services.
   - Link domestic Trips.

3. **Finance**
   - Approve revenue and cost lines.
   - Record vendor invoices and customer invoices.
   - Record receipts and payments.
   - Close shipment financials.

4. **Branch manager**
   - Approve exceptions, discounts, credit limits, and write-offs.

5. **Head office**
   - View and report across branches.
   - Manage shared masters and cross-branch controls.

Important actions should require approval history rather than a simple editable status.

## 17. Non-negotiable correctness rules

- A shipment is not the same thing as a domestic Trip.
- A container or package is not the same thing as a shipment.
- A master bill can have many house bills.
- Totals must be calculated on the server.
- Branch ownership must be derived from the authenticated user or parent record.
- Closed shipments should not be silently edited; use controlled amendments.
- Documents must retain history and verification state.
- Financial approval must be auditable.
- Deleting a shipment should be restricted once financial or customs records exist.
- All list, detail, export, and API queries must apply branch authorization.

## 18. Recommended shipment financial model

### 18.0 Scope clarification: domestic Trips are unchanged

The current domestic Trips module is already considered acceptable and is not part of
this shipment implementation.

- Do not change the existing domestic Trip workflow.
- Do not add shipment vendor accounting into domestic Trip screens.
- Do not require a domestic Trip when creating an import/export shipment.
- Do not copy domestic Trip fields into the shipment vendor model.

The new work is limited to the import/export shipment workflow and its shipment-side
vendor records. The vendor association is primarily an operational and historical
record: it tells GTS which supplier handled which shipment service.

### 18.1 Do not store vendor costs only inside the shipment creation form

The shipment creation form should capture the initial commercial estimate, but vendor costs should be entered as separate cost transactions linked to the shipment.

This is safer than placing every vendor cost directly on the shipment because:

- One shipment can use several vendors.
- One vendor can issue several invoices.
- An estimated cost can differ from the final invoice.
- A cost may be paid in installments.
- Finance may need to approve or reject a cost.
- The shipment must retain an audit trail when a cost changes.

The shipment should therefore have:

1. **Estimated cost lines** for planning.
2. **Approved vendor cost lines** for financial control.
3. **Vendor bills/payables** for amounts actually owed.
4. **Vendor payments** for amounts actually paid.

### 18.2 Customer receivable model

The customer side should use a separate receivable workflow:

```text
Shipment
  -> Customer charge lines
  -> Customer invoice
  -> Customer receipt/payment
  -> Receivable balance
```

Customer charges may include:

- Freight
- Customs clearance
- Documentation
- Port or airport handling
- Delivery
- Warehousing
- Local transport
- Government charges recharged to the customer
- Other service fees

The invoice must show the customer as the party from whom money is receivable. A receipt must be linked to the customer invoice and shipment, not merely entered as an unexplained payment.

Vendor identity and vendor internal notes must never be copied to the customer invoice
by default.

### 18.3 Vendor payable model

Vendor work should use this workflow:

```text
Shipment
  -> Vendor service request
  -> Estimated vendor cost
  -> Approved vendor cost
  -> Vendor bill
  -> Vendor payment
  -> Payable balance
```

Each vendor cost should contain:

- Shipment
- Branch
- Vendor
- Service category
- Related leg, container, package, house bill, or domestic Trip when applicable
- Description
- Currency
- Exchange rate
- Estimated amount
- Approved amount
- Final invoiced amount
- Tax or VAT
- Vendor invoice/reference number
- Approval status
- Payment status
- Due date
- Supporting document
- Created by and approved by

The vendor bill must appear in the vendor payable area and in the shipment financial tab. It must not be added to the customer invoice unless a separate customer charge is created to recharge that cost.

### 18.4 Vendor classification

A vendor should have a master classification describing the services it normally provides:

- Shipping line
- Airline
- Customs broker
- Port or terminal
- Warehouse
- Transport provider
- Overseas agent
- Insurance provider
- Other supplier

“Payable” and “receivable” should not be treated as a permanent vendor type. They are transaction directions:

- A vendor cost is normally payable.
- A customer charge is normally receivable.
- A credit note, refund, commission, or vendor rebate may create an amount receivable from a vendor.

If the business later works with the same company as both a customer and a supplier, use a shared party/company master with separate customer and vendor roles, rather than duplicating the company or changing its type manually.

### 18.5 Shipment vendor record: the simple required workflow

For the first shipment implementation, vendor handling should work as follows:

1. The user creates an import/export shipment.
2. On the shipment, the user opens **Shipment Vendors**.
3. The user selects an existing vendor.
4. The user selects the service type:
   - Shipping line
   - Airline
   - Customs broker
   - Port
   - Warehouse
   - Transport provider
   - Overseas agent
   - Insurance provider
5. The user records the vendor reference, service description, and dates.
6. The system saves the vendor association on the shipment.
7. The shipment displays the vendor in its internal operations history.
8. The vendor page displays the shipment under that vendor's handled jobs.
9. The customer invoice remains unchanged and does not show this vendor record.

The first version may record a vendor cost for internal reference, but it must keep
these concepts separate:

- **Vendor association:** who handled the service.
- **Vendor cost:** what GTS expects or owes for that service.
- **Customer charge:** what GTS chooses to bill the customer.

Adding a vendor association must never automatically create a customer invoice line.
Adding a vendor cost must also never automatically create a customer invoice line.
Only a deliberate **Add to customer charges** action should create a customer charge,
and that action should record the user, time, source vendor cost, and approval.

### 18.10 Domestic Trip cost treatment

A domestic Trip linked to a shipment should remain a Trip record, but its financial effect should be visible in the shipment.

Recommended behavior:

- The Trip keeps its driver, truck, fuel, route, delivery note, and trip payment details.
- The Trip link identifies the shipment, house bill, container, or delivery leg it serves.
- The shipment receives an allocated operational cost from the Trip.
- If the Trip is billed to the customer, a separate customer charge is created.
- If the Trip is an internal fleet movement, it contributes to shipment cost but is not automatically added to the customer invoice.

This prevents duplicate entries while still producing accurate shipment profitability.

### 18.11 Profit calculation

Use two clearly named profit views:

#### Estimated profit

```text
Estimated customer revenue
- Estimated vendor and operational costs
= Estimated gross profit
```

#### Actual profit

```text
Customer invoiced revenue excluding VAT
- Approved/final vendor costs
- Domestic Trip operational cost
- Government/pass-through cost treated as expense
= Actual gross profit
```

VAT collected or recoverable should not be treated as profit. Foreign-currency amounts should be converted using the stored transaction exchange rate, and both original and base-currency values must be retained.

### 18.12 Example scenario

For shipment `GTS-IMP-SEA-2026-0001`:

1. Sales creates a shipment for Ocean Gate.
2. Sales adds estimated customer revenue:
   - Freight: OMR 800
   - Customs clearance: OMR 150
   - Delivery: OMR 100
3. Operations books:
   - Shipping line: estimated cost OMR 550
   - Customs broker: estimated cost OMR 90
   - GTS domestic delivery Trip: estimated internal cost OMR 60
4. Finance approves the commercial and vendor amounts.
5. The shipping line sends a bill for OMR 575.
6. The customs broker sends a bill for OMR 90.
7. The customer invoice is issued for OMR 1,050 plus applicable VAT.
8. Finance records:
   - OMR 300 customer receipt
   - OMR 575 vendor payment
9. The system shows:
   - Customer receivable: OMR 750 plus any unpaid VAT
   - Vendor payable: OMR 90
   - Shipment actual cost: OMR 725 plus the allocated domestic Trip cost
   - Actual gross profit based on invoice revenue and approved/final cost

The vendor bills never become customer invoice lines automatically. If the business wants to recover a vendor cost from the customer, the user must explicitly add a customer charge linked to that cost.

## 19. Inline vendor capture during shipment creation

The preferred user experience is a single shipment form with an internal **Vendor
for this shipment** section.

The user may:

1. Select an existing active vendor from the current branch.
2. Choose the shipment service category.
3. Enter shipment-specific vendor details.
4. Or select **Create new vendor** and enter the vendor master details inline.
5. Save the shipment once.

The server must save the operation in one database transaction:

```text
Shipment form submission
  -> Validate shipment fields
  -> Find selected vendor in the authenticated branch
  -> Or create a new Vendor in the authenticated branch
  -> Create shipment-vendor assignment
  -> Create shipment charge lines
  -> Commit all records together
```

If any part fails, no partial shipment or vendor assignment should remain.

### 19.1 Existing vendor versus new vendor

#### Existing vendor

The form submits:

- `vendor_id`
- `service_category`
- Shipment-specific vendor fields

The system must verify that the vendor belongs to the authenticated branch and is
active. It must not trust a vendor ID from another branch.

#### New vendor

The form submits a nested vendor payload, for example:

```text
vendor_mode = new
vendor.vendor_code
vendor.company_name
vendor.vendor_type
vendor.country
vendor.city
vendor.contact_person
vendor.email
vendor.phone
vendor.currency
vendor.payment_terms
vendor.bank details
```

The server creates the vendor once and uses its ID for the shipment assignment. The
vendor master is not duplicated into the shipment record.

The inline form should not require a vendor invoice attachment when the shipment is
being created. A vendor invoice is a later finance document and should be uploaded
only when received.

### 19.2 What is stored

The vendor master stores reusable company information:

- Company identity
- Vendor code
- Contact details
- Address
- Bank details
- Currency and payment terms
- Vendor type
- Active status

The shipment-vendor assignment stores only this shipment's relationship:

- Shipment
- Vendor
- Service category
- Booking/reference number
- Service description
- Requested/confirmed dates
- Service status
- Internal notes
- Optional estimated/final cost

This is the correct normalization: one vendor can serve many shipments, and one
shipment can use many vendors.

### 19.3 Invoice privacy rule

The vendor section is internal-only. It must not be rendered on customer invoices.
Customer invoice lines come only from explicitly approved customer revenue lines.

The vendor assignment can be visible to:

- Sales, if authorized
- Operations
- Finance
- Branch managers
- Head-office users

It must not be visible to the customer through invoice PDFs, customer portals, or
customer-facing shipment documents.

## 20. Existing-code and structure audit

### 20.1 Existing strengths

- `Vendor` already uses branch scoping through `BelongsToBranch`.
- `VendorController` validates vendor codes as unique within a branch.
- Existing vendor master fields cover company, contact, bank, currency, and payment
  terms.
- Vendor activation/deactivation already exists.
- `ShipmentController` already uses a database transaction for shipment creation
  and shipment charge synchronization.
- Shipment types and customer/sales-person selection already exist.
- Existing charge lines already separate buy and sell amounts.

### 20.2 Current gaps and risks (rechecked 28 September 2026)

1. Shipment records still live in `transport_records`, which also stores domestic
   Trips. Shipment finance, status, and vendor records reference that shared table.
2. Shipment numbers are generated from the latest row for the branch and type;
   they do not yet use a year-keyed atomic sequence counter. Concurrent creation can
   produce a duplicate.
3. Status history exists, but the workflow does not yet enforce a complete
   role-aware transition matrix, document gates, or operational/financial close
   rules.
4. Shipment status history and vendor-bill audit exist, but a general append-only
   shipment activity history is not yet comprehensive across edits, documents,
   customer receipts, vendor payments, cancellations, and exceptions.
5. Shipment appointments are not modeled as scheduled records. A port appointment
   charge is only a financial amount; it does not reserve a time slot.
6. Container and package fields are not yet normalized into shipment cargo records
   with assignment and lifecycle history. Container reuse and duplicate active-use
   rules need definition.
7. Document upload exists, but a type-specific checklist, version history,
   verification, and enforced clearance/delivery requirements are incomplete.
8. Customs/clearance task management, shipment-to-domestic-Trip linking, and a
   Shipment -> Trip -> Delivery Note handoff are incomplete.
9. Currency and exchange-rate fields exist in parts of the finance model, but a
   consistent per-line OMR base amount, rate direction/date/source, and immutable
   approved-rate snapshot are not enforced everywhere.
10. Some shipment, invoice, bill, and history foreign keys cascade on delete.
    Posted finance and audit records must be protected before allowing shipment
    deletion or moving shipment rows to a new table.
11. Invoice synchronization can create a draft invoice while a shipment is being
    read. Review this side effect and make finance-document creation explicit or
    otherwise safely idempotent and transactionally controlled.
12. Vendor assignments currently synchronize an automatic draft bill and prevent
    a second active bill for the same assignment. Supporting multiple bills per
    assignment requires an explicit partial-billing and cost-allocation design.
13. Holds/exceptions, ETA/ETD changes, demurrage/detention deadlines, delivery
    proof, and operational reminders need a coherent owner-and-history workflow.
14. Branch scoping is present in shared model/controller patterns, but every
    shipment child, finance lookup, export, route binding, and cross-branch
    exception still needs a systematic authorization audit.

### 20.3 Recommended implementation order

The authoritative, staged implementation plan is Section 21. It intentionally
starts by protecting the live finance workflows, then adds numbering and workflow
controls, then migrates shipment storage, and only then expands the operational
workspace. Do not implement the old sequence of adding a second set of invoice,
bill, or payment tables: reuse and evolve the working shipment finance models
unless a documented migration proves a replacement is required.

### 20.4 Important structural recommendation

Do not create a second vendor table. Extend the existing vendor master and add a
shipment-vendor relationship table.

Do not copy all vendor bank and contact fields into every shipment. Store a
historical snapshot only when legally or operationally required, such as the vendor
name and reference shown on an internal shipment record.

### 20.5 Implemented foundation and remaining work

Already implemented in the current application:

- Shipment types stored in `transport_records`, with branch-aware list/workspace
  access patterns.
- Shipment-vendor assignments, branch-scoped vendor selection, inline vendor
  creation, and vendor history.
- Shipment charge lines that keep customer sell charges separate from vendor
  assignments and buy costs.
- Customer invoices, approval/issue workflow, invoice email/PDF, receipts, and
  Accounts Receivable.
- Vendor bills, approval, audit events, invoice-document reconciliation, payments,
  statements, and Accounts Payable.
- Invoice adjustments for post-issue charge changes.
- Shipment status history and basic operational status updates.

These are existing foundations, not a reason to skip hardening. The remaining

### 20.6 Verification fixtures

Local verification data can be loaded with:

```bash
php artisan db:seed --class=ShipmentDemoSeeder
```

The seeder uses clearly identifiable `DEMO-` values and creates one branch-scoped
customer, vendor, driver, truck, import shipment, and shipment-vendor assignment.
It is repeatable and updates the same demo records instead of creating duplicates.
It should be used only for local verification, not as part of a production seed.

The fixture belongs to the `GT-Line` branch and uses the dedicated local account
`demo.gtline@example.test` with password `password`. Import/export records are
excluded from the domestic Trips query; the Trips module accepts only records whose
`trip_type` is `domestic` or unset.

The shipment workspace is available at `/shipment/{shipment}` and is branch-scoped.
For example, the GT-Line demo shipment must be opened while authenticated as the
GT-Line demo user; a GTS-branch user correctly receives a not-found response.

The first customer-billing slice is now available from the shipment workspace.
After a shipment is delivered, completed, or closed, finance can create one draft
customer invoice from its sell-side shipment charges. Invoice lines are copied as
historical snapshots. Vendor assignments, vendor costs, internal notes, and profit
are never copied into the customer invoice.

The Charge Master is the controlled rate catalog for shipment services. Staff should
normally select a Charge Master item on the shipment form so the standard buy rate,
sell rate, VAT, job type, and government-charge flag are loaded consistently. A
manual charge remains available only for an approved exception or a service that
has not yet been added to the catalog. The invoice uses the resulting sell amount,
not the vendor cost.

Charge Master job type filtering is intentional: a charge for `import_air` is not
shown on a `transit` shipment. Charges shared by multiple shipment types should be
created with job type `all`; type-specific charges should be created separately.
The shipment's customer invoice currency is selected on the shipment form and is
used consistently for its customer charge lines. Master rates are starting values,
not a currency conversion. Vendor assignment currency defaults from the selected
vendor and can be overridden for that shipment.

The Oman logistics catalogue is seeded as inactive templates with stable codes and
zero rates. The seeder only inserts missing codes, so it does not overwrite rates or
branch applicability. To activate currently inactive rows without changing their
rates or applicability, run
`php artisan db:seed --class=ActivateInactiveChargeCatalogSeeder` in the intended
environment. Review that database first because this seeder activates every
currently inactive charge. Zero-rate entries remain marked **Rate required**;
prices are not invented. Government charges are billed at cost. The catalogue
source is recorded as `Oman Logistics Charges.pdf`.

FCL means **Full Container Load**: one shipment uses a whole shipping container.
LCL means **Less than Container Load**: cargo shares container space with other
shipments and is commonly charged by volume (CBM), weight, or a consolidation
handling unit. Sea shipments separately record import/export type and FCL/LCL
classification. The charge catalogue is filtered by both fields, and server-side
validation rejects mode or FCL/LCL mismatches. FCL/LCL-specific charges are rejected
until a shipment classification is selected.

Customer and vendor statements keep balances separate by currency. No unlike
currencies are added together unless an exchange rate is explicitly recorded.

Customer charges and vendor services are separate records:

```text
Charge Master -> shipment charge line -> customer invoice (sell amount)
Vendor assignment -> vendor cost/booking -> future vendor bill (buy amount)
```

Selecting a customer charge does not automatically create a vendor assignment. This
prevents a customer invoice line from incorrectly becoming a vendor payable. The
operator must assign the actual shipping line, airline, broker, port, warehouse, or
other vendor separately on the shipment.

The shipment create and edit screens use the same customer-charge workflow. The
legacy flat financial fields (freight/container rent, customs duty, clearing
charges, documentation charges, and transit bond fee) are no longer presented as
shipment charge inputs. Existing database columns are retained for backward
compatibility, but new customer billing must be entered through Charge Master
lines so it has one auditable source for invoicing.

Finance can open the invoice review page and move it through the controlled internal
states `Draft -> Approved -> Issued`, or cancel it. Sending email, PDF generation,
and customer payment allocation remain the next billing phase.

The Accounts sidebar entry remains responsible for internal Trip and Maintenance
payment vouchers. Those vouchers are submitted, sent for admin approval, and their
PDF is unlocked after approval. Shipment vendor bills are separate Accounts
Payable documents and are reached through **Vendor Bills / Payables**.

A shipment is the source context for a vendor bill, so the professional workflow is
to create the bill from the shipment workspace (or choose a shipment from the
Payables screen), not from an unrelated Trip voucher form.

Each vendor bill has two identifiers:

- **Internal AP number**: generated automatically, for example `AP-2026-0001`.
- **Vendor invoice number**: the number printed by the vendor, entered when the
  vendor invoice is received and optionally left blank while the bill is drafted.

This separation prevents users from manually inventing internal payable numbers
and preserves the vendor's original invoice reference for reconciliation.

Shipment status is updated from the shipment workspace using the shipment-specific
status selector. The current status, location, user, timestamp, and optional note
are recorded in `shipment_status_histories`. The domestic Trips status endpoint and
its `Pending/Loaded/In Transit/Reached/Completed` workflow remain separate.

The shared legacy `transport_records.trip_status` column retains the domestic Trip
values and has been expanded to include the shipment status values. This is required
because both modules currently use the same legacy table; the controllers still
validate and expose separate status workflows.
### Shipment customer receipts (implemented)

Shipment customer invoices use the branch-scoped `shipment_invoice_receipts` ledger. Receipts can
only be posted against issued, sent, overdue, or an already partially-paid invoice; the invoice is
locked while posting so the paid and balance amounts cannot race or exceed the invoice total.
The Accounts Receivable screen lists outstanding invoices, marks issued/sent invoices overdue after
their due date, and links to the receipt form. Each shipment invoice also displays its complete
receipt history. This workflow is separate from domestic trip `CompanyPayment` and
`PaymentAllocation` records.

### Finance statements and branded documents (implemented)

Customer and vendor Statements of Account are available under the Accounts menu. Each statement is
branch-scoped, supports an optional customer/vendor and date range filter, shows a running balance,
and can be downloaded as a branded A4 landscape PDF. Customer statements show invoices and receipts;
vendor statements show approved vendor bills and payments. Draft and cancelled vendor bills are
excluded from payable statements.

Customer invoice email delivery uses a server-generated branded PDF attachment. It is only available
for issued, sent, overdue, or partially-paid invoices and requires a valid customer email address.
Vendor invoice documents can be uploaded to a vendor bill, downloaded by finance users, and
reconciled after the bill is approved and the vendor invoice number is recorded. Vendor documents
and costs remain outside customer-facing invoice PDFs and emails.

## 21. Consolidated professional roadmap and end-to-end workflow

This section is the current implementation plan. If an older “current gaps” or
“deferred” list elsewhere in this document conflicts with Section 20.5 or this
roadmap, the implementation status in Section 20.5 and the sequence below take
precedence. The plan extends the working finance workflows; it does not start a
parallel accounting system.

### 21.1 Design rules

1. **Keep Trips and Shipments distinct.** Domestic Trips remain responsible for
   vehicles, drivers, fuel, trip payments, and Delivery Notes. A Shipment is the
   freight job and can exist without a domestic Trip.
2. **Reuse the live finance ledgers.** Evolve the current shipment invoices,
   invoice lines, receipts, vendor bills, vendor bill payments, adjustments,
   statements, and vendor-bill audit. Do not create duplicate AR/AP tables unless
   a migration plan explicitly replaces, reconciles, and retires an existing
   ledger.
3. **Make branch ownership authoritative.** Derive a child record's `branch_id`
   from its parent shipment and validate every submitted customer, vendor, user,
   leg, container, Trip, invoice, and bill against that branch. Cross-branch
   access requires an explicit head-office permission and policy.
4. **Keep posted finance immutable.** A draft may be corrected or, under a
   documented retention policy, soft-deleted. Once an invoice or bill is approved,
   issued, or paid, never hard-delete it or silently rewrite posted values. Use a
   cancellation/void with a reason or a credit/debit adjustment, with an audit
   event. Soft deletion by itself is not an audit trail.
5. **Keep histories append-only.** Record who did what, when, in which branch,
   and why. Retain status, finance, document, appointment, container, and exception
   events when a draft or shipment is cancelled or archived.
6. **Calculate money on the server.** Browser values are previews only. Persist
   original amount and currency, exchange-rate snapshot, rate direction/date/source,
   and OMR base amount for every financial line. Define rounding and VAT behavior
   and freeze the approved/issued snapshot.
7. **Separate operational and financial state.** Operations may be closed while
   collection or payment remains outstanding. Financial close is a separate,
   controlled action and may require all invoices, receipts, vendor bills,
   payments, adjustments, and reconciliations to be resolved.
8. **Represent events as records.** A milestone records that an event occurred;
   an appointment reserves a time window. Do not model either as a miscellaneous
   text field on the shipment.

### 21.2 Business rules to confirm before implementation

The following are proposed defaults for review before coding:

| Decision | Proposed default |
|---|---|
| Shipment number format | `GTS-IMP-SEA-2026-0001` or `GTL-EXP-AIR-2026-0001`; branch + type + year + sequence |
| Number sequence | One counter per branch, shipment type, and year; reset each year; never reuse a number already issued, even after cancellation |
| Branch currency | OMR base/reporting currency; persist source currency and the exact rate snapshot used |
| Exchange-rate direction | OMR per one unit of source currency, with rate date and source recorded |
| Container duplicate rule | A physical container can be reused on later shipments; block overlapping active assignments according to the agreed operational scope and retain history |
| Vendor assignment billing | Keep current one-active-bill behavior until partial bills, cost allocation, and reconciliation are specified; then support one assignment to multiple bills intentionally |
| Draft deletion | Soft-delete or cancel drafts only where no posted/paid finance or protected audit records would be lost |
| Posted document correction | Explicit cancel/void reason or credit/debit adjustment; never hard-delete approved, issued, or paid records |
| Operational / financial close | Independent states; operational close does not imply invoices are fully paid |
| Shipment-to-Trip creation | Explicit user action after required local-delivery details are confirmed; never create a Trip or Delivery Note as a hidden side effect |

Changing any default requires updating this table and its acceptance tests before
the affected phase starts.

### 21.3 Target end-to-end shipment flow

1. **Create the job.** Sales or authorized operations staff create a draft in the
   correct branch and select shipment type, customer, customer reference, parties,
   cargo mode, and initial route. The system allocates the next branch/type/year
   shipment number transactionally. A shipment does not require a domestic Trip.
2. **Quote and confirm commercial terms.** Add customer revenue charges from the
   Charge Master or an authorized manual exception. Store source currency, rate
   snapshot, VAT, and server-calculated OMR values. Keep customer charges separate
   from vendor service requests and costs.
3. **Book vendors and transport legs.** Add one or more vendor assignments and
   shipment legs. Record the responsible vendor/carrier, booking reference, service,
   planned dates, route, and operational owner. Vendor bank/contact data remains
   in the vendor master; internal vendor information is never copied to a customer
   invoice.
4. **Schedule appointments.** Create port/terminal, yard gate-in/out, pickup,
   delivery, customs inspection, warehouse handoff, or empty-return appointments.
   Store the time window and timezone, location, reference, responsible party,
   owner, status, and reschedule/cancellation history. Link an appointment to the
   relevant leg and, when needed, multiple containers through a linking table.
   Reminders and overdue indicators go to the assigned owner.
5. **Register cargo and documents.** Record containers or packages as distinct
   shipment cargo items; associate them with the applicable house bill and leg.
   Track container lifecycle events and timestamps (for example, gate-in, loaded,
   departed, arrived, gate-out, delivered, and empty-returned) without making a
   container number globally unique forever. Upload versioned, verified documents
   against a checklist tailored to shipment type and the next status transition.
6. **Clear customs and manage exceptions.** Operations records clearance tasks,
   declaration/reference numbers, inspection result, duty/tax, release status, and
   supporting evidence. A hold/exception has a category, owner, reason, expected
   resolution date, updates, and a resolved timestamp. Status transitions that
   require missing documents, failed inspection, or unresolved blocking exceptions
   are rejected server-side unless an authorized, audited override is used.
7. **Create a domestic delivery Trip when needed.** When GTS performs local
   collection or delivery, an authorized user explicitly creates or links a
   domestic Trip to the Shipment and relevant leg/container/house bill. The Trip
   keeps its own driver, truck, route, costs, payment, and status. Its Delivery
   Note remains linked to the Trip and is exposed from the Shipment workspace.
   Shipment vendor accounting is not duplicated in Trip accounts.
8. **Meet operational invoice readiness.** The system shows which required
   operational checks remain (for example, delivery proof, customer charges, or
   required documents). Authorized finance staff can create/review the customer
   invoice from approved sell-side charges. Reading a list or detail page should
   not unexpectedly create or alter financial documents.
9. **Issue customer invoice and collect AR.** Finance approves and issues the
   invoice; sent/email/PDF events are retained. Receipts are posted to the invoice
   with transaction locking and overpayment protection. Statements and aging use
   the existing branch-scoped Accounts Receivable workflow.
10. **Approve vendor costs and pay AP.** Vendor estimates remain distinguishable
    from final costs. Reconcile actual vendor invoices and documents, approve bills,
    and record one or more payments under the agreed bill-allocation rules.
    Statements and aging use the existing branch-scoped Accounts Payable workflow.
    Vendor costs do not become customer revenue unless an explicit customer charge
    is created.
11. **Close in two dimensions.** Operations may close after operational work and
    required evidence are complete even when AR/AP remain open. Finance closes only
    after defined invoice, receipt, bill, payment, adjustment, and reconciliation
    criteria pass. Freeze a profitability snapshot at financial close; correct it
    later only through an audited amendment or adjustment.
12. **Retain the record.** Search, dashboards, reports, exports, audit history, and
    document downloads remain branch-authorized. Cancelled/closed shipments and
    posted finance history remain available under the retention policy.

### 21.4 Staged implementation plan and exit gates

#### Stage 0 — Rules, inventory, and baseline

- Confirm the proposed rules in Section 21.2 with Sales, Operations, Finance, and
  branch management.
- Inventory all shipment-related tables, foreign keys, route bindings, jobs,
  reports, attachments, and production data before schema changes.
- Map current invoices, invoice lines, receipts, vendor assignments, vendor bills,
  bill payments, adjustments, audits, and statements to their owning shipment.
- Record baseline tests and branch-specific counts/financial totals for migration
  comparison.

**Exit gate:** approved business-rule table; reviewed FK/data map; repeatable
baseline checks; no production data cleanup hidden inside a schema migration.

#### Stage 1 — Protect existing finance and audit history

- Remove unsafe hard-delete/cascade paths for shipments with protected history.
- Define draft soft-delete/cancel behavior and prevent deletion of approved, issued,
  partially paid, paid, reconciled, or audited finance records.
- Require a reason and authorized actor for cancellation/void and preserve issued
  invoice or bill snapshots.
- Expand append-only activity events to cover shipment edits, charge changes,
  status changes, documents, invoices, receipts, vendor bills/payments, adjustments,
  holds, and overrides.
- Review and remove write side effects from shipment GET/list/detail requests.

**Exit gate:** tests prove protected finance and audit rows survive cancellation
and shipment deletion attempts; every sensitive action records actor, branch,
timestamp, event, and reason; GET requests do not create finance documents.

#### Stage 2 — Numbering, branch authorization, and workflow controls

- Add an atomic sequence table keyed by branch, shipment type, and year, protected
  by a unique key and transaction/row lock. Never determine the next number solely
  by scanning the highest shipment row.
- Preserve already issued numbers; sequence gaps from cancelled jobs are allowed
  and numbers are never recycled.
- Enforce a role-aware status transition matrix, cancellation/reopen policy, and
  separate operational and financial statuses.
- Add document/readiness gates and a permissioned, audited exception override.
- Verify route binding, child creation, exports, search, and finance actions are
  branch-safe.

**Exit gate:** parallel creation tests produce unique numbers; branch-crossing
requests are denied; invalid state transitions and missing-document transitions
are rejected; allowed transitions create immutable history.

#### Stage 3 — Separate shipment storage from domestic Trips

- Introduce the dedicated shipment domain table/model and compatibility mapping
  while keeping `transport_records` as the domestic Trip table.
- Use an expand/backfill/verify/cutover migration: add the new schema, backfill
  existing shipment rows, compare counts and financial relationships, dual-read or
  compatibility-test as needed, then switch routes and services.
- Migrate/update foreign keys for charges, assignments, invoices, lines, receipts,
  vendor bills, payments, adjustments, status histories, and audits without losing
  identifiers or statements.
- Keep existing route URLs working during rollout or provide redirects; provide a
  tested rollback/restore procedure.
- Do not combine destructive table removal with the first production cutover.

**Exit gate:** pre/post row counts, branch ownership, invoice/bill balances,
receipt/payment totals, documents, and representative URLs reconcile exactly;
Trips and shipment dashboards show only their intended record types.

#### Stage 4 — Cargo, legs, appointments, and documents

- Normalize legs, containers, packages, and bills; link containers/packages to
  the correct house bill and leg. Add linking tables only for genuine many-to-many
  relationships.
- Track container assignment and status history, including empty return. Enforce
  the agreed no-overlapping-active-use rule while permitting future reuse.
- Add appointment and appointment-resource/container links, timezone-aware windows,
  booking references, owners, status history, rescheduling, cancellation reasons,
  reminders, and overdue views.
- Add type-specific document checklists, versioned upload history, verification,
  expiry, and required-document gates.
- Add customs clearance tasks and inspection outcomes.

**Exit gate:** tests cover appointment conflicts/reschedules/timezones, container
reuse and active overlap, document version history and status gates, branch access,
and customs hold/release.

#### Stage 5 — Shipment-to-Trip operational handoff

- Add explicit shipment/leg/container/house-bill to domestic Trip links with
  purpose, planned/actual dates, status, and audit history.
- Provide a user-confirmed “Create domestic Trip” action that pre-fills only
  validated shipment details and still requires Trip-specific driver, truck, and
  delivery information.
- Link the Trip's Delivery Note back through the Trip relationship and show its
  status/document in the Shipment workspace.
- Keep domestic Trip payment, driver, truck, and operating-cost ledgers authoritative
  in the Trip module.

**Exit gate:** one shipment can link to the required number of Trips; duplicate
links are controlled; Trip/Delivery Note changes appear in Shipment history; no
duplicate trip payments or vendor costs are created.

#### Stage 6 — Currency, approvals, and financial close

- Complete source-currency, exchange-rate direction/date/source, OMR base amount,
  decimal precision, and VAT rules across charges, customer invoice lines, vendor
  bill lines, receipts, and payments.
- Calculate and validate conversion server-side; snapshot approved/issued rates;
  define how later FX differences are represented rather than silently rewriting
  historical amounts.
- Decide whether one vendor assignment can produce multiple bills. If partial
  billing is needed, add explicit bill items/allocations, cumulative cost checks,
  duplicate invoice controls, and reconciliation rules first.
- Define operational close, financial close, partial close, reopening, adjustment,
  and profit snapshot policies.
- Add “Ready for Invoice” checks using configured operational evidence and charge
  approval, while retaining finance approval as a separate permissioned action.

**Exit gate:** currency and rounding tests match approved examples; invoice/bill
totals reconcile to lines and payments; posted snapshots cannot be edited; close
eligibility and amendments are auditable.

#### Stage 7 — Reporting, notifications, and controlled expansion

- Build branch dashboards for upcoming appointments, late ETA/ETD, missing
  documents, customs holds, empty returns, overdue customer invoices, vendor
  invoices/payments, and low/negative margin.
- Route notifications to accountable owners with configurable thresholds and
  deduplication; avoid broad noisy alerts.
- Add explicitly authorized head-office cross-branch views and export audit.
- Add retention, backup/restore, and operational audit export procedures.

**Exit gate:** dashboard totals reconcile to source records; notifications are
deduplicated, permission-safe, configurable, and tested; branch users cannot obtain
cross-branch data through filters, exports, or direct URLs.

### 21.5 Rollout and production safety

- Deliver one stage at a time behind tested migrations and reversible application
  cutovers where possible.
- Back up production and test restore before any shipment data migration.
- Run migrations separately from code deployment; do not seed demo data in
  production.
- Use production-like data volumes and both GTS and GT-Line test accounts.
- After each stage, smoke-test shipment creation/edit, customer invoice/receipt,
  vendor bill/payment, statements, Trips, Delivery Notes, branch restrictions,
  document downloads, and reports.
- Do not proceed to the next stage if financial totals, branch ownership, or
  foreign-key relationships fail reconciliation.
